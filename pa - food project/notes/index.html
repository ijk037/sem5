Executive Synopsis

proem

Calibrating procurement in rapid-dispatch culinary ecosystems demands an intersection of botanical precision and stochastic rigor. Across multi-region ghost kitchens, perishability constraints penalize both stock depletion and over-preparation with extreme asymmetry.

This monograph presents a dual-tier forecasting framework tailored for 77 regional commissary units. By isolating recurring weekly cycles from exogenous promotional interventions and transient climate anomalies, our models yield dependable forward distributions that honor both cold-room storage limits and kitchen staff capacity curves.

Methodology Stages

stepwise modeling architecture

1

Exploratory Rhythm Analysis

Data Discovery

Uncovering weekly seasonality, holiday shocks, and elasticity across 77 fulfillment centers. We observed pronounced mid-week lull patterns succeeded by high-amplitude dinner surge spikes on Fridays and Sundays, necessitating independent calendar fixed effects.

2

Count & Linear Formulations

Parametric Baselines

Applying Poisson generalized linear models (GLM) and regularized Ridge regressions to calibrate base rate order flows. These simple regularized estimators provided an interpretable lower-bound benchmark, effectively penalizing extreme promotional coefficient swings.

3

Tree-Based Gradient Boosting

Nonlinear Core

Training LightGBM on log1p-transformed target volumes with rolling momentum lags (orders_lag_1w, rolling_mean_4w). Feature importance analysis confirmed that recent hub-level moving averages accounted for over 44% of directional split gain.

4

Forward SARIMA Projections

Stochastic Horizon

Fitting (0,1,2)(1,0,1)[4] multiplicative seasonal ARIMA models for long-horizon kitchen fleet provisioning. The residual autocorrelation tests (Ljung-Box p > 0.05) validated complete white-noise innovation, ensuring reliable 10-week safety stock confidence intervals.
